The speaker runs **ESPHome 2026.9 or later on ESP-IDF 6.1,** which the ESP32-S31 requires.

## Components

- **Sendspin** for synchronized multi-room audio.
- Gigabit Ethernet through the YT8531 PHY.
- Two TAS58xx amplifier instances, one per chip.
- A Bluetooth proxy, plus the S31's own radio for Thread.
- Stock components for the temperature sensor, the radar and the I2S mics.

## The amp driver fork

The base driver (mrtoy-me's esphome-tas58xx) already has per-channel 15-band EQ, mixer
routing, gain, BTL/PBTL mode and fourth-order filter presets. But its presets only cover
60-150 Hz, which suits a sub and nothing else. The fork adds:

- **Linkwitz-Riley (LR4) low-pass and high-pass presets at 1.5, 2, 2.5 and 3 kHz.**
  Each is two cascaded Butterworth biquads in the chip's fixed-point coefficient format
  at 48 kHz, generated by a script and checked at -6 dB at the crossover frequency. Bench
  status: not yet measured.
- **Band-pass presets for the woofer**: a 2 kHz low-pass plus a high-pass around 100 Hz.
- **Presets and EQ bands together on one channel.** The base driver only allows one or
  the other.

## Boot order

Getting this wrong can cost a tweeter, so the order is fixed:

1. The amps are held off by hardware (shutdown pin pulled low) until the S31 is running.
2. The amp chip won't accept EQ writes without a stable I2S clock, so the firmware plays a
   half-second boot sound first.
3. **The tweeter high-pass and the sub limiter load before anything is unmuted.** Both are
   stored in flash and reloaded at every boot.
4. The limiter level is set from the PoE class the switch granted, read from the
   class-status optocouplers.

## What the hardware asks of the firmware

- Hold the PHY in reset for at least 10 ms, and set the 2 ns RGMII delays in its registers.
- Drop the link to 100 Mbit/s and slow the S31 clock when idle, to save power.
- Lower the sub limiter when amp A raises its over-temperature warning.
- Use spread spectrum and offset the two amps' phase, for EMC.
- Handle LLDP power requests, and stay above PoE's minimum current so the switch doesn't
  drop the port.

## What Home Assistant sees

Temperature, humidity, presence and target position, sound level, link speed, sled
temperature, whether the sled is seated, amp faults and warnings, amp sleep state, and
the watts negotiated with the switch.
